What Happens When ATS Reads a Canva Resume

Applicant Tracking Systems are not humans. They are text parsers — software that reads your document like a machine, extracting raw text and mapping it to data fields: Name, Email, Company, Job Title, Dates, Skills.

When you design a resume in Canva and export it as a PDF, Canva generates the file using a combination of graphic layers, SVG vector text, CSS multi-column layouts, and embedded image elements. To a human eye, it looks clean and structured. To an ATS parser, it looks like a scrambled mess.

⚠️ What the ATS actually sees: Instead of "Software Engineer at Google, 2022–2024," the parser might extract "Software at Engineer Google 2022 2024" — or nothing at all, if the text is locked inside a vector graphic layer.

The 4 Specific Ways Canva Breaks ATS Parsing

  1. Multi-column layouts. Canva's most popular templates use 2-column designs (sidebar with skills/contact, main column with experience). ATS parsers read left-to-right, top-to-bottom — like a typewriter. A 2-column layout causes the parser to interleave your sidebar text with your experience section, creating gibberish.
  2. Text inside graphics. Canva frequently renders text as vector graphics (SVG paths) rather than selectable, copyable text. The ATS treats these as images — completely invisible to the parser.
  3. Fancy fonts rendered as images. Custom Canva fonts that aren't system fonts are often baked directly into the PDF as image layers. Your name, your email, your contact info — all invisible to ATS.
  4. Headers and footers. Canva templates love decorative headers with contact info in the top banner. Many ATS systems skip the header/footer area of PDFs entirely, meaning your phone number and email are never parsed.

The "But My Resume Looks Correct When I Download It" Problem

📌 Critical insight: The fact that your Canva PDF looks correct when you open it in Adobe Reader is completely irrelevant to ATS compatibility. ATS systems do not "view" your PDF — they parse the raw text stream underneath. A PDF can look perfect visually while being 100% unparseable by an ATS.

Think of it like a beautiful shop window covered in photos of products, but the actual store inside is completely empty. The ATS is trying to walk into the store — it does not care what the window looks like.

You can test this yourself right now: open your Canva PDF, press Ctrl+A (select all), then Ctrl+C (copy), then paste into Notepad. Whatever text you can copy is roughly what the ATS can extract. If huge chunks of your resume are missing, they are invisible to ATS.

What To Use Instead: The ATS-Safe Alternatives

✅ Option 1: Google Docs (Free, Safest)

Google Docs with a clean, single-column template is the most ATS-compatible option available. Key rules:

  • Use a single-column layout only. No sidebars.
  • Use standard fonts: Arial, Times New Roman, Calibri, Helvetica, or Georgia.
  • Put contact info in the body of the document, not in a header or text box.
  • Export as .docx (not PDF) when possible — DOCX is parsed more reliably than PDF by 90% of ATS systems.
  • Avoid tables for layout — use tab stops or paragraph indents instead.

✅ Option 2: Microsoft Word (Best for DOCX)

Microsoft Word's built-in resume templates are specifically designed to be ATS-compatible. Stick to the "simple" or "chronological" templates. Avoid any Word template that uses text boxes, SmartArt, or multiple columns for layout.

The Canva Workaround (If You Must Use Canva)

If you have a Canva resume you love and absolutely must use it, here is the safest workaround — though we strongly advise against it for job applications at large companies:

  1. Download as PDF. Not PNG, not PowerPoint.
  2. Copy-paste test. Select all text (Ctrl+A), copy, paste into Notepad. Review what was captured.
  3. Manually fill the ATS form. When the ATS application form has text fields for Work Experience, Skills, etc., fill those out manually even though you are also uploading the PDF. The manual fields always override the parsed resume in final scoring.
  4. Use Canva for human-reviewed applications only. Recruiters at creative agencies, design studios, or startups who review resumes manually will appreciate the design. For corporate or tech roles, switch to a clean template.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can ATS read Canva resumes?
Most ATS systems cannot reliably read Canva resumes. Canva exports text inside graphic layers, SVG elements, and multi-column CSS layouts that ATS parsers cannot interpret. The result is garbled, incomplete, or entirely missing text in the recruiter's system.
Will a Canva resume get rejected by ATS?
Yes — a Canva resume is very likely to receive a low ATS score or be filtered out automatically. Even a highly qualified candidate can be rejected simply because their template prevented the ATS from reading their experience and keywords.
What is the best resume format for ATS?
A clean, single-column .DOCX file is the gold standard. If you need to submit a PDF, ensure all text is selectable (not rendered as graphics). Use standard fonts, avoid tables and text boxes, and keep your contact info in the document body — not in a header.
Does Canva have an ATS-friendly resume template?
Canva has introduced some "ATS-friendly" templates, but they still export as PDFs with limitations. Even their simplest templates often use multi-column elements or graphic text rendering that causes parsing issues. For guaranteed ATS compatibility, use Google Docs or our free ATS Resume Builder.
Is a PDF or DOCX better for ATS?
DOCX is generally safer. About 90% of ATS systems parse DOCX more reliably than PDF. The exception is if you are uploading to a company that specifically requests PDF. If given no instruction, send DOCX and offer to provide PDF separately if requested.
